* PVS
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: pvs
:END:

#+ATTR_HTML: :class tool-purpose
Specification, proof, and executable models

#+begin_toolgrid
#+begin_toolcopy
A specification language and interactive prover for mathematical theories,
hardware, and software systems.

- *Specify:* higher-order logic, predicate subtypes, dependent types, datatypes,
  and parameterized theories.
- *Prove:* induction, rewriting, decision procedures, and reusable strategies;
  integrated Yices SMT reasoning and experimental BDD/CTL model checking.
- *Execute:* ground evaluation and PVSio. Research extensions PVS2C/PVS2Rust
  generate C/Rust; they are *not part of the public PVS release*.
- *Reuse:* libraries and theory interpretations. PVS complements SAL's
  transition-system analysis; SAL is a separate tool.

*Use it for:* controller invariants, processor correctness, distributed
algorithms, and formalized mathematics.

#+ATTR_HTML: :class tool-resources
[[https://pvs.csl.sri.com/][Website]] · [[https://pvs.csl.sri.com/documentation.html][Documentation]] · [[https://github.com/SRI-CSL/PVS][Source / releases]]
#+end_toolcopy

The quantified theorem states monotonicity. Typechecked; one subtype obligation

* Yices
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: yices
:END:

#+ATTR_HTML: :class tool-purpose
Constraint solving for verification and synthesis

#+begin_toolgrid
#+begin_toolcopy
An SMT solver that checks whether constraints can hold together and produces
satisfying models when they exist.

- *Model:* arithmetic, bitvectors, arrays, tuples, scalar types, and
  uninterpreted functions. MCSat-enabled builds add nonlinear arithmetic.
- *Query:* incremental contexts, assumptions, models, and unsatisfiable cores;
  support varies by logic and solver configuration.
- *Integrate:* native Yices or SMT-LIB input, a C API, and Python, Java, Go,
  and OCaml bindings. Yices also supports PVS and SAL reasoning workflows.

*Use it for:* overflow checks, hardware equivalence, program analysis,
synthesis, and scheduling constraints.

#+ATTR_HTML: :class tool-resources
[[https://yices.csl.sri.com/][Website]] · [[https://yices.csl.sri.com/yices2-documentation.html][Documentation]] · [[https://yices.csl.sri.com/doc/yices-language.html][Language]] · [[https://github.com/SRI-CSL/yices2][Source / releases]]
#+end_toolcopy

* SAL
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sal
:END:

#+ATTR_HTML: :class tool-purpose
A suite for concurrent-system analysis

#+begin_toolgrid
#+begin_toolcopy
The Symbolic Analysis Laboratory applies complementary analysis engines to
one compositional transition-system language.

- *Model:* typed state, initialization, guarded transitions, and module
  composition; =sal-wfc= provides limited typechecking.
- *Check:* BDD-based =sal-smc=, SAT-based =sal-bmc=, and SMT-based
  =sal-inf-bmc=. Bounded checking finds bugs; induction can establish invariants.
- *Explore:* deadlock detection, counterexamples, and =sal-atg= test generation;
  experimental simulation, explicit-state, and witness tools.
- *Connect:* Yices backs bounded checking; HybridSAL abstracts hybrid systems
  into SAL. Sally is a separate model checker, not the SAL suite.

*Use it for:* protocols, mutual exclusion, and fault-tolerant distributed
algorithms, under explicitly modeled assumptions.

#+ATTR_HTML: :class tool-resources
[[https://sal.csl.sri.com/][Website]] · [[https://sal.csl.sri.com/documentation.shtml][Documentation]] · [[https://sal.csl.sri.com/download.shtml][Source / downloads]] · [[https://sal.csl.sri.com/introduction.shtml][Suite guide]]
#+end_toolcopy

* CHERI
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: cheri
:END:

#+ATTR_HTML: :class tool-purpose
Architecture · Capability-based memory protection and compartmentalization

#+begin_toolgrid
#+begin_toolcopy
CHERI extends processor instruction sets with hardware-enforced capabilities.
Developed through the SRI–University of Cambridge collaboration, it brings
protection into the hardware–software interface.

- *Constrain:* capabilities carry an address, bounds, and permissions. Hardware
  tags distinguish valid capabilities from ordinary data; software cannot forge
  authority by constructing an arbitrary bit pattern.
- *Protect:* accesses through capabilities are checked against their bounds and
  permissions. Compilers, runtimes, and operating systems use these mechanisms
  for fine-grained memory protection.
- *Compartmentalize:* software can delegate limited authority to components,
  reducing what a compromised component can access. The design of interfaces
  and the supporting software remains part of the security argument.

*Use it for:* memory-safe systems software and components with restricted access
to shared resources. Temporal safety requires supporting revocation mechanisms;
capabilities alone do not eliminate every class of vulnerability.

#+begin_details Radler — Multi-Rate Integration Architecture
*Description*

Radler implements a formally grounded approach to designing, developing, and
verifying distributed multi-rate cyber-physical systems, ensuring robustness,
safety, and performance in critical applications.

- Multi-rate model of computation
- Publish-subscribe communication architecture
- Quasi-periodic execution
- Bounded-latency channels

*Purpose*

Radler provides strong architectural guarantees for safety, security,
resilience, which are formally proven in PVS.

- Certifiable to a range of assurance levels
- Seamless integration with easy SW/HW-in-the-loop testing and debugging, and
  rapid prototyping.

*Use Cases*

Critical cyber-physical systems including drones, robots, medical devices,
SCADA, autonomous systems, and agentic AI.

*Download*

Radler is available from the [[https://github.com/SRI-CSL/radler][Radler GitHub repository]] under the
[[https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-3.0.html][GPL v3]] license.

Licenses for commercial usage or specific support are available upon request.
#+end_details

#+begin_details ETB — Evidential Toolbus
*Description*

Distributed framework for integrating various software systems analysis and
verification tools. Designed to facilitate the continuous creation and
maintenance of assurance cases, automatically updating claims and evidence as
system requirements or components change.

*Purpose*

The primary purpose of ETB is to significantly reduce the cost, labor, and
/ad hoc/ nature of developing and maintaining assurance cases for critical
systems. By automating the evidence generation and argument construction
processes, it aims to:

- Provide /accountable evidence/ that corroborates the safety and other
  critical properties of software.
- Enable /continuous assurance/ for complex systems, where traditional manual
  processes are unsustainable.
- Improve /rigor and trustworthiness/ of assurance cases by grounding them in
  formal logic and verifiable evidence.

*Use Cases*

Automated Driving, Flight-Control Systems, Software System Certification,
AI/ML Assurance, Continuous Authority to Operate, Software Understanding.

*Download*

The latest ETB2 is available from the [[https://github.com/SRI-CSL/etb2][ETB2 GitHub repository]] under the
[[https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-3.0.html][GPL v3]] license.

Licenses for commercial usage or specific support are available upon request.
#+end_details

#+begin_details Assurance 2.0 — Rigorous Assurance Cases
*Description*

Modern approach to the development and assessment of assurance cases, which
are structured arguments backed by evidence to demonstrate that a system
possesses certain critical properties (e.g., safety, security).
[[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.10665][Assurance 2.0]] builds upon the established Claims, Arguments, Evidence
methodology but introduces a higher degree of rigor and systematic evaluation:

- Logical Soundness of the decomposition of claims into subclaims
- Identification, analysis, and refutation of potential defeaters (challenges
  or doubts) to the argument
- Probabilistic assessment of residual risks
- Confirmation theory: does evidence support a claim? how well does it
  discriminate against alternative claims?

*Purpose*

Provide a robust framework for justified confidence in the deployment of
complex, critical systems, especially those incorporating new technologies
like AI and autonomous capabilities, where traditional testing alone is
insufficient.

*Use Cases*

Certification of Safety-Critical Systems, Security Assurance, AI Assurance,
Regulatory Compliance, Continuous and Incremental Assurance.

*Tool Support*

The Assurance 2.0 methodology is usually implemented in industry-specific
development frameworks, with [[https://github.com/SRI-CSL/etb2][ETB]] used for curating evidence in CI/CD
pipelines. We have been cooperating with Adelard (now part of [[https://www.nccgroup.com/][NCC group]]) in
developing /Clarissa/, which directly supports Assurance 2.0.
#+end_details

#+begin_details PCE — Probabilistic Consistency Engine
*Description*

Probabilistic inference for Markov Logic Networks, which combine first-order
logic with Markov networks. PCE relies on sampling-based methods such as Gibbs
sampling to approximate marginal and conditional probabilities and it uses
maximum a posterior inference, allowing it to achieve scalability for larger
problems.

*Purpose*

Enable logical reasoning about systems where uncertainty and probabilities are
inherent. This is crucial for analyzing and verifying systems where components
might fail with a certain probability, or where decisions are based on
uncertain inputs. It helps in modeling and analyzing probabilistic systems,
consistency checking of probabilistic statements, and probabilistic inference.

*Use Cases*

Probabilistic program analysis, information extraction and integration,
knowledge base construction, natural language processing, semantic parsing,
social network analysis, computer vision, computational biology, robotics and
autonomous systems, fraud detection, cybersecurity.

*Download*

PCE is available from the [[https://github.com/SRI-CSL/pce][PCE GitHub repository]] under the
[[https://www.gnu.org/licenses/lgpl-3.0.html][LGPL v3]] license.
#+end_details
